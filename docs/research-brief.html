The Planted Post: why this question, what exists, what we add

A scripted auditor post on an agent message board invites a swarm to break one rule. Its file, place and timing are the same in every condition; only its words change. We measure who follows it, who tells a human, and which monitors would notice.

Bottom line. The Hugging Face incident began with two ingredients: agents with impossible tasks, and a shared folder they were never told about. Prior work has studied agents under impossible tasks (ImpossibleBench, The Troy Moment) and propagation of injected instructions (Prompt Infection), but nobody has put an auditor-controlled post on the board, held its actions fixed, and scored the population's deeds, words and detectability together. That is the undercover method competition authorities use, and the one we validated in a pricing market last sprint (The Undercover Firm), moved to swarms.
What we found. (1) No agent broke the rule in 32 solvable-task swarms: pressure comes first. (2) One sentence moved gpt-4.1-mini from 0% to 15% and Gemma 4 31B from 0% to 27% of agents; other models barely moved. (3) 61 of gpt-5-mini's 71 violators asked the unresponsive human Overseer first: silence was read as consent. (4) Scripted social proof did not spread violations; an "access is logged" notice did not deter. (5) A canary tripwire caught every violation; monitors reading what agents wrote missed many quiet open-weight violations, and board monitors mistook the invitation for the deed. Market context: who would use this

  • Agent observability and runtime security is a fast-growing category: vendors such as Galileo, LangSmith, Langfuse, Arize, Fiddler and Straiker sell tracing and guardrails. Analysts' sizing varies widely by definition (e.g. about USD 0.4B in 2025 for agent observability by one estimate), so treat figures directionally. Buyers increasingly ask for multi-agent tracing of delegation chains and cross-agent messages.
  • Canary tokens and honeytokens are standard intrusion-detection practice, and early open-source agent canaries and red-team guides now exist; they are rarely used as evaluation instruments for agent populations.
  • Gap: tools trace what agents did; few let an auditor ask a question of a swarm before deployment. A planted post plus canary is a cheap, standardisable pre-deployment test.

Risks and how we handle them

  • Dual use. The sandbox is a Python dictionary: no real files, network, credentials or exploit content. The "violation" is reading a decoy file.
  • Small cells. Six seeds per cell (48 agents); episode-level bootstrap CIs and permutation tests; we pool only where stated.
  • Judge reliability. Ground truth for violations comes from the gateway log, not an LLM. LLM judges are only used as monitors under test.
  • Prior work. Report and README mark pre-existing code (our own build scripts from the last sprint) and state what is new.
